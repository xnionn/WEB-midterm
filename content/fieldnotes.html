Note 01 / On getting started / 3 min read

The blank page
isn’t a test.

There’s a particular kind of hesitation that arrives with a new notebook. The first mark suddenly feels far too important.

Give the page a smaller job. It doesn’t have to hold your best idea. It can hold five lines, a rubbing of a leaf, or a list of things you noticed on the way home.

A useful constraint makes the first move easier: one colour, two minutes, only shapes with corners. Choose a rule that feels playful, then let the page surprise you.

When the timer ends, resist deciding whether the result is good. Ask a more useful question: what would I like to try next?

Keep a little curiosity

Three notes to open.

Click a title. Follow a thought.

02Collect a colour, not a photo.Observation+

Choose one colour before a short walk. Notice where it turns up: a door, a thread in someone’s coat, the edge of a receipt. Describe three examples in words when you get back.

The point is to pay attention without turning everything into an image to keep. “Warm orange, like the inside of a pencil” may tell you more than another photograph.

Try it: Find five shades of one colour in ten minutes.

03A tiny book can hold a big question.Paper experiment+

Fold one sheet of paper into quarters. Each panel becomes a small place for an answer. Start with a question you can’t settle in one sentence: what makes a place feel like home?

Use a word, a shape, a memory, and an empty space. Different kinds of answers can sit beside each other. The small format makes it easier to finish without explaining everything.

Try it: Make a four-panel book about one ordinary object.

04Let the wrong line stay.A making habit+

Drawing in pen leaves a record of your decisions. The line that went too far can become a shadow, a border, or the beginning of something else.

For one sketch, leave the eraser out of reach. This isn’t about proving you can draw perfectly. It’s a way to practise responding to what is already on the page.

Try it: Draw your mug without lifting the pen. Keep every line.
